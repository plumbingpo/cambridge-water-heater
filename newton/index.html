Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Newton

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
