Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Somerville

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Hot Water Headaches Somerville Homeowners Face

A water heater in Somerville almost never fails without warning. Look out for a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, brownish hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. These are early signs of sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and when they're caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
