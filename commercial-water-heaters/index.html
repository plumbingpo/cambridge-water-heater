Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A commercial heater that has grown noticeably louder
  • Unsteady delivery temperatures that suggest a failing mixing valve
  • Lukewarm water at the exact time you need the most
  • Dish or hand sink temperatures drifting below what your inspector expects
  • Rust-colored water, leaks at fittings, or moisture under the heater
  • Rising gas or electric bills with no change in business volume
  • A recirculation loop that no longer seems to do its job
  • A heater old enough that a failure feels like a matter of time

Tanks rarely fail all at once. They corrode from the inside for months, and the warning signs show up long before the floor gets wet. If you notice any of these signs, contact Cambridge Water Heater at (617) 209-6487 for a prompt inspection.

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

The scale of commercial equipment changes the job. Gas input is often several times that of a home unit, which affects gas line sizing, meter capacity, combustion air, and venting. Electric models can draw enough current to require substantial service capacity. Storage often runs at 140 degrees or higher, so thermostatic mixing valves are needed to deliver safe water at fixtures, and those valves must be matched to the flow and tested. Recirculation loops need the right pump, working check valves, and balancing, or hot water takes the easy path and distant fixtures stay cold.

On top of the technical side come code and timing. Commercial installations usually involve permits, inspections, and sometimes additional requirements for larger vessels, and a job that fails inspection can leave a business without hot water for days. Our experienced technicians coordinate those steps before the old unit comes out, schedule work around your operating hours, and stage swaps on multi-unit systems so you are never fully without hot water. That planning is where the real value lies: the right equipment, installed to code, on a timeline that protects your revenue.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Verify delivery temperatures at sinks and showers a few times a year
  • Keep the area around the heaters clear for combustion air and service access
  • Replace consumed anode rods before the tank itself starts to corrode
  • Keep recirculation pumps and check valves in service to avoid long waits and wasted energy
  • Log water temperatures at key fixtures so drift is caught early
  • Flush high-demand heaters on a regular interval to keep recovery strong
  • Plan replacement before an aging unit fails during peak season
