Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Medford

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away as soon as something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Medford

Most water heater failures we see in Medford start with the water itself. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be a real factor: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, insulating the water from the burner, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping you may hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why regular descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

Then there's age. When the protective anode rod is gone, often well before the tank itself looks old, the steel begins corroding from within, and a drip from the bottom seam often shows up next. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Code requirements add another layer. Swapping out an older heater often means bringing the install up to current code: an expansion tank if the plumbing is a closed system, a relief valve drain line piped to an approved spot, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, and a drain pan or seismic straps where required. Well water brings its own challenges, such as heavier sediment, iron staining, and the sulfur odor that occasionally shows up when certain water chemistry reacts with a standard anode rod. All of these can be fixed, and most are much cheaper when caught early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Call (617) 209-6487 and we'll take a look.
