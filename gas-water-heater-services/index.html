No hot water from a gas heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Here in Cambridge, MA, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across Cambridge, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Safety always comes first: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and Cambridge Water Heater can take over from there.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank
  • Hot water that runs out faster than it used to
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door

Why Hire a Professional for Gas Water Heater Services?

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. A pilot outage might come from a worn thermocouple, a partly blocked pilot tube, lint on the flame arrestor screen, a downdraft, or an internal valve failure. Guess wrong and you have spent money without fixing anything. Guess wrong on a gas fitting or vent connection and the stakes are much higher, since a small leak or a spillage problem can put gas or carbon monoxide into the living space. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically call for a permit.

Professional service gets you a correct answer and a safe appliance. We confirm thermocouple output, gas pressure, burner flame, and draft with real instruments, not trial and error. Every gas connection we make is tested for leaks before we leave, and vent connectors are secured with the right slope and clearances. When a replacement is needed, we size the new heater by first-hour rating, handle the permit where one is required, and add the expansion tank, sediment trap, drain pan, or seismic straps your home needs. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
