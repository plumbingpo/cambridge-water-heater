Tankless units are clever machines, and that is both their strength and their weak spot. Instead of keeping forty or fifty gallons hot around the clock, a tankless heater springs into action only when water starts moving: a flow sensor detects demand, the control board fires the burner or elements, and water is heated on its way through the heat exchanger. Every step is watched by a sensor, and if any reading drifts outside its expected range, the board shuts the heater off and displays a code. For Cambridge, MA homeowners, that usually traces back to a short list of causes: scale building up inside the heat exchanger, a sticking or dirty flow sensor, an ignition problem at the igniter or flame rod, or a venting or gas supply issue the unit reads as unsafe.

Cambridge Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Cambridge, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat shows up clearly in those numbers. Error codes narrow the search, but they rarely name the broken part outright. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

After the fault is pinned down, most repairs are finished in a single visit. We descale heat exchangers with a pump and descaling solution circulated through the service valves, replace worn flow sensors, igniters, flame rods, thermistors, and combustion fans, clean inlet filter screens, and correct venting problems that trip pressure switches. When the real issue is a gas line sized for the old tank heater, we tell you plainly and quote the correct pipe run, because no amount of part swapping will fix a unit that is starved for fuel. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater
  • Longer bursts of cold water between hot draws than you used to get
  • A heater that only fires when water runs at full blast
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

There are two reasons tankless repair belongs with a professional: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. A mistake with either one is not a small mistake.

A trained technician with proper test equipment gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. Descaling is done with a circulating pump and the proper solution, followed by a thorough rinse. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, you will hear it from us before you spend money on a repair.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Clear debris, snow, and dryer lint away from the vent and intake openings
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • If scale comes back quickly, add a softener or a scale-prevention cartridge on the cold inlet
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
