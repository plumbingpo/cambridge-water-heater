Upfront Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Before we start any repair, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians look at the age and size of the tank, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. After that you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

Most water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Keep an eye out for discolored or rusty hot water, popping noises when the burner fires, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a spent anode rod, or a failing dip tube. An early service call at the first sign often costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is nearly always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap with water on the floor. Our technicians know how local water conditions affect tank life, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Annual Flushing

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

A maintenance visit from Cambridge Water Heater is more than a quick drain and refill. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, tests the elements and thermostats on electric units, and sets a safe water temperature. Small problems get caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and the heater keeps running at full efficiency.

Many homes do best with one flush a year, and houses on hard water often need two. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household works it, and the mineral content of your water, which determines how fast sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

Think of maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already bought. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod working, and the relief valve ready to open, and that lowers monthly energy bills while reducing wear on the burner or elements. For most households, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Proper Sizing

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one wastes energy keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Cambridge Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means matching the first-hour rating to your morning rush, rather than simply copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute to the fixtures you run at once and the temperature of your incoming water.

Needs change over time. A new baby, a teenager, or an in-law suite can overwhelm the tank that used to be plenty. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as you stay within the rated flow, while hybrid heat pump tanks draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll lay out the real trade-offs in installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Repairs We Handle

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Cambridge

Homes around Cambridge run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Cambridge Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The right repair depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes get solved with an expansion tank plus a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and we handle leak repairs at the drain valve and fittings before the damage spreads. We also verify the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
